ESIEE Paris × Sopra Steria · Team of three · 2026

Five-year hourly demand coverage and grid constraint analysis in Mauges Communauté.

Capstone project sponsored by Sopra Steria: how far can solar PV and wind capacity expand in Mauges Communauté without breaching grid hosting limits or incurring excessive curtailment?

Why this study?

Matching renewable generation and demand hour by hour

Mauges Communauté hosts 232 MW of wind and solar PV capacity, generating an annual volume equivalent to roughly 42% of local electricity consumption. However, annual energy balance masks hourly mismatches—raising critical questions around surplus generation, curtailment, and distribution substation hosting capacity.

Proposed by Sopra Steria within its smart grids and energy storage practice, the brief required evaluating expansion pathways and quantifying flexibility levers. Our three-student team modelled five full years of operation (2021–2025).

Methodology

Reconstructing hourly profiles and comparing pathways

Public datasets at the inter-municipal scale did not directly provide complete hourly time series. We reconstructed hourly load, generation, and weather profiles to simulate system behaviour across every hour of the five-year period.

Our interactive web simulator lets users adjust installed PV/wind capacities, load assumptions, and demand-response levers. While reconstructed demand profiles illuminate system-level trajectories, they complement—rather than replace—detailed substation connection studies.

  • Reconstructed and validated hourly time series spanning 43,824 hours (2021–2025).
  • Benchmarked multiple solar PV and wind capacity expansion scenarios.
  • Modelled demand-side flexibility by shifting domestic hot-water tank heating to solar peak hours.
  • Enforced a 5% annual curtailment cap and assessed primary substation hosting capacities.

Key outcome

Higher hourly coverage without utility-scale battery storage

41.6% → 55.4%simulated hourly electricity demand covered under the recommended pathway

Our recommended roadmap pairs a +25% increase in solar PV and +50% in wind capacity with midday load shifting for domestic water heaters. In our simulation, this strategy raises hourly demand coverage to 55.4% without electrochemical storage while keeping curtailment below the 5% threshold in each of the five years analysed.

Achieving this trajectory requires siting new projects on primary substations with remaining hosting headroom, within the modelling assumptions used to reconstruct local hourly demand.

Deliverables

A technical report and an open web simulator

The report documents input datasets, reconstruction methods, scenario trade-offs, and our final recommendations. The public web simulator allows stakeholders to test parameters interactively.
